# Fatefall roadmap — 100 tasks

A working list. Tick items as they land; keep the numbering stable so commits can cite them
(`roadmap #12`). Groups are ordered roughly by what the game gains soonest.

## A. The party and its people (1–10)

- [x] 1. Chronicle page: each member's road, title, and the deeds journal on one screen.
- [x] 2. Party banter on the map: short exchanges between two members triggered by room kinds and mood.
- [x] 3. Bonds: pairs who fight together often gain a small bonus when adjacent in the line.
- [x] 4. Grudges: a member who was downed by a monster kind gets +1 to hit it thereafter. (already in as vendettas)
- [x] 5. Member epitaphs: a fallen member's name, level and killer recorded and shown at the title.
- [x] 6. Hirelings with contracts: guards hired in town leave after N floors and want pay.
- [x] 7. Mounts on the overworld: a horse bought in town speeds travel and can be lost to a storm.
- [x] 8. Familiars and pets: a wizard's cat, a ranger's hound, drawn beside them and worth one Perception reroll.
- [x] 9. Party name generator with themes, and renaming from the Chronicle.
- [x] 10. Retirement: a level-20 member can retire to a town as a quest giver.

## B. Combat depth (11–25)

- [x] 11. Reactions: shield block, parry, counterspell as real reactions with one use per round.
- [x] 12. Opportunity attacks shown as their own beat in the presenter.
- [x] 13. Cover: pillars and water in the battle scene grant +2 AC to the side behind them.
- [x] 14. Flanking: two allies adjacent to a foe give advantage. (already in)
- [x] 15. Monster morale with bands: when the leader falls, the rest check or flee. (already in)
- [x] 16. Boss phases: at half health a boss changes tactics and the backdrop shifts.
- [x] 17. Environmental attacks: lair actions that use the room's feature (the altar, the forge).
- [x] 18. Conditions on the sprite: poisoned green tint, frightened shiver, paralysed freeze.
- [x] 19. Damage types on the numbers: fire orange, cold blue, radiant gold, with a matching hit sound.
- [x] 20. A "hold" order: the DM tells a member to hold their action until a condition.
- [x] 21. Ranged weapons with range bands and a penalty at the far band.
- [x] 22. Ammunition: arrows and bolts counted, restocked in town, running out mid-fight.
- [x] 23. Two-weapon fighting and shields as real choices on the equip pass.
- [x] 24. Combat summaries: "the fight in numbers" line after each victory.
- [x] 25. Difficulty setting on the party builder: story, normal, hard, with monster HP and DC scaling.

## C. Magic (26–35)

- [x] 26. Ritual casting out of combat: detect magic on a chest, identify at camp.
- [x] 27. Concentration: one concentration spell at a time, broken on damage by a Con save. (already in: concentration breaks on damage)
- [x] 28. Spell scrolls learnable by wizards at camp.
- [x] 29. Cantrip scaling by level, shown in the spell list. (already in: cantrip dice by level)
- [x] 30. Summons: a druid's beast or a wizard's elemental as a temporary combatant.
- [x] 31. Wild magic surges for sorcerers, with a table of twenty results.
- [x] 32. Warlock pacts with a patron who asks for things in dreams.
- [x] 33. Spell components: a rare spell needs an item the party must find.
- [x] 34. Counter-magic from monsters: casters that dispel the party's buffs.
- [x] 35. A grimoire page in the compendium for every spell, with its element and delivery.

## D. Items and economy (36–45)

- [x] 36. Item rarity tiers with colour and price, and sets that give a bonus when worn together.
- [x] 37. Cursed items with a removal quest, not only a temple fee.
- [x] 38. Crafting: the forge and the town smith turn monster parts into gear.
- [x] 39. Monster parts as loot: hides, fangs, glands, sold or crafted.
- [x] 40. Weight and encumbrance: a laden party moves slower on the overworld.
- [x] 41. Shops with stock that changes with the calendar and the town's mood.
- [x] 42. Haggling: a Charisma check on big purchases.
- [x] 43. A bank in the capital: deposits survive a lost party in hardcore.
- [x] 44. Legendary items with names, histories, and a card when found.
- [x] 45. Trophies: a boss's head mounted in the tavern, with a reputation bonus.

## E. The world (46–60)

- [x] 46. Weather that affects the dungeon: rain floods the first floor, snow slows the road.
- [x] 47. Seasons that change the overworld palette and the markets.
- [x] 48. Roads that improve as towns prosper, and decay when they are raided.
- [x] 49. Town sieges: a monster band camps outside a town until the party breaks it.
- [x] 50. Town growth: quests done raise a town's tier and unlock buildings.
- [x] 51. A capital city with a court, a bank, and quests from the crown.
- [x] 52. Ships: a port town sells passage to a far coast with its own gates.
- [x] 53. Named rivers with fords and bridges the party must find.
- [x] 54. Ruins that can be claimed as a base with a chest and a bed.
- [x] 55. Wandering merchants with rare stock on the road, on a schedule.
- [x] 56. Overworld fog of war that remembers what was seen, with a map screen.
- [x] 57. A world map screen with pins for gates, towns, and quest targets.
- [x] 58. Points of interest that chain: the witch sends you to the grove, the grove to the tomb.
- [x] 59. Regional bosses that roam the overworld and can be tracked.
- [x] 60. Festivals with games on the tray: archery, wrestling, a race.

## F. Dungeons (61–72)

- [x] 61. Locked doors and keys carried by a named monster on the floor.
- [x] 62. Secret doors found by Investigation, leading to a hidden room with a hoard.
- [x] 63. Multi-floor pre-built dungeons with a set-piece: a bridge, a drowned hall, a throne.
- [x] 64. Dungeon maps as loot: a map reveals the floor.
- [x] 65. Light: torches burn down; darkness gives monsters advantage until relit.
- [x] 66. Shrines with a choice: a boon for a cost, remembered by the deity.
- [x] 67. Traps that the party can set: caltrops, a tripwire in a corridor.
- [x] 68. Dungeon factions: two monster bands at war, the party can side with one.
- [x] 69. A rescue floor: prisoners in cells scattered across a floor, all to be freed.
- [x] 70. Bosses that talk: a line before the fight and a line at half health.
- [x] 71. Vaults that need three keys from three floors.
- [x] 72. Dungeon "weather": spore clouds, cold snaps, gas that fills a room over rounds.

## G. Story (73–80)

- [x] 73. Act interludes: a card between acts telling what the antagonist did meanwhile.
- [x] 74. Companions from the story: an NPC who joins for one act.
- [x] 75. Multiple endings by flags, with an epilogue card per member.
- [x] 76. Story choices with delayed consequences: a spared enemy returns later.
- [x] 77. Rumours that foreshadow the next act's antagonist by name. (taverns already name the act's villain; the interlude card adds to it)
- [x] 78. A villain's lieutenant on the floor before the boss.
- [x] 79. The Chronicle records choices with what they cost.
- [x] 80. New Game Plus: a finished story reseeds with the old party's titles remembered.

- [x] 91. Standing orders: "never pay tolls", "always parley", "loot everything".
- [x] 92. Waypoints: the DM marks a tile and the party goes there.
- [x] 93. A DM notebook: free-text notes that ride in the save.
- [x] 94. Command history with up-arrow recall in the DM box.

- [x] 97. Speed presets bound to keys, and pause on any card.
- [x] 98. A statistics screen: rolls, crits, kills by kind, gold over time.
- [x] 99. Export the run as a text chronicle the player can share.
- [x] 100. Achievements: forty of them, with a card and a title when earned.
